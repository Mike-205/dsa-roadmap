# Pass 5: Stress-Test

**Read this after attempting the four checkpoints and, ideally, after working through M0–M15's friction problems.** Like `04-curriculum.md`'s own "Checkpoint answer keys" section, this document discusses milestones' and checkpoints' intended reasoning directly — reading it first would spoil exactly what it's checking for.

Goal (per `00-philosophy.md`): does this curriculum teach transferable reasoning about unfamiliar problems, or is it a disguised LeetCode roadmap in different clothes? Pass 4 proposed the four checkpoint projects as the primary instrument, with a caveat already flagged there: they're authored by the same person who is the self-directed learner, which threatens to defeat them as blind tests for that person.

## Method

Running the "test" empirically isn't possible — nobody has taken this curriculum yet. Pass 5 is therefore a design audit, not an empirical one, and it only means something if failure is a real possible outcome. Three falsifiable signatures of "disguised LeetCode," decided before auditing:

1. **Friction problems that name the fix.** Per Kapur's actual condition (`01b-gap-check.md` line 9, verified above, not recalled): the learner must attempt a problem *before* being shown the technique. A milestone where the remedy is already stated — whether inside the Friction problem field's own text, or in an Intuition/Formal field positioned above it in the same milestone, with nothing in the document instructing the learner to attempt before reading — has forfeited Kapur's demonstrated transfer advantage for that unit specifically. This isn't a Kirschner-Sweller-Clark violation (KSC favors guided instruction, and a leaked fix is guided instruction by another name) — it's a narrower claim: the curriculum invoked Kapur's condition explicitly (04's own cross-cutting mechanisms section) and needs to actually meet it to collect the benefit Kapur's research reports.
2. **Blocked vs. interleaved practice.** Outside the four checkpoints, every implementation task sits inside the milestone that just taught its technique. This is reasonable, unremarked-upon blocked practice for the skill-building phase — the KSC/Hmelo-Silver exchange (`01b-gap-check.md` line 11) is about guided vs. unguided *instruction*, not blocked vs. interleaved *scheduling*, so it doesn't license or condemn this choice either way. What does matter: if the *only* points requiring the learner to choose unprompted among multiple already-taught techniques are the four checkpoints, that's a thin interleaving layer — and pushing the one external, untagged practice source (AoC) to the very end makes it thinner than Pass 1c intended.
3. **Transfer distance per checkpoint.** None (restates a task or reading the learner has already done under different words) / near (recognizable as a recent milestone's technique, no leaked citation) / far (requires synthesizing multiple milestones' content into something not explicitly taught).

A fourth check, explicitly assigned to this pass by `01b-gap-check.md` (line 15: *"Pass 5's stress-test should check for this explicitly: does every unit have a consolidation step, not just a struggle step?"*), isn't a failure signature — it's a direct instruction to verify. Answered in Finding 0, before the three signatures above, since it's a precondition for taking any of them seriously.

A curriculum that fails signature 3 on most checkpoints has a stress-test layer that can't detect its own failures on signatures 1–2 either, which is why 3 is audited first.

## Finding 0 — Every milestone has a labeled consolidation step (Pass 1b's explicit check)

Grepped all 16 milestones for a labeled Formalization field. **Pass: 16 of 16** (M0–M15) have one — M6's is worded "Formalization, precise about what's derived vs. cited:" rather than the plain label, but it's the same field, and its extra qualifier is a strength, not a gap (it explicitly separates the derivable O(log n) bound from the cited O(α(n)) one rather than blurring them). **One internal inconsistency, not a failure of the check:** M8 packs four nodes (binary search, insertion sort, divide & conquer, comparison lower bound) into one milestone, and only one of the four — Comparison lower bound — gets its own explicitly labeled "Formalization:" field; binary search and insertion sort rely on "Formal:" citation fields and a cross-link to divide & conquer's recurrence instead of a restated consolidation step in the friction→formalization shape used everywhere else. The content is real (the O(log n) proof is genuinely there, cross-linked), just not uniformly labeled for a milestone this crowded. Worth tidying if M8 is ever split, not worth blocking on.

## Finding 2 — Friction problems that name the fix

Two separate checks, not one. The narrow one (does the Friction problem field's own text state the remedy) is what the first draft of this pass checked. The broader one (does *anything read before the Friction problem, in the same milestone*, already state the remedy) is what actually determines whether Kapur's condition holds, since 04's template lists Intuition/Formal/Problem fields above each milestone's Friction problem, and nothing in 04 — before this pass's fix, below — told the learner not to read them first.

**2b — the structural gap.** Even the milestones with no 2a leak (M2, M10, M14, M15) place their Intuition/Formal fields — which name real techniques (M2: MIT 6.006's doubling argument, line 86; M10: AVL "framed as fixing unbalanced-BST degradation," line 217; M14: memoization lectures, line 282) — *above* the Friction problem field in reading order, with nothing in 04 instructing the learner to attempt before reading further. This resolves 2b as a rule; it doesn't retroactively fix the 6 milestones in 2a, where the remedy leaks even under that rule since it's stated inside or immediately adjacent to the friction prompt itself (listed as an open item below).

## Finding 3 — Interleaved practice is thin, and was made thinner by a Pass 4 drift

Outside the four checkpoints, every implementation task in M0–M15 sits inside the milestone that just taught its technique — that's 16 milestones of blocked practice against 4 points (the checkpoints) requiring the learner to select among multiple already-taught techniques unprompted. That ratio is a real, structural risk for the LeetCode-pattern-matching failure mode `00-philosophy.md` names, independent of any single checkpoint's contamination.

**Correction to Pass 4, verified against the primary source:** `passes/01c-gap-check-2.md` (line 43) states Advent of Code should be introduced "**late, after arrays-through-graphs are covered**, since later days assume the full toolkit." Pass 1c predates this project's milestone numbering, so "arrays-through-graphs" is an interpretation, not a literal milestone citation — but under any reasonable reading it means after the graph milestones, i.e. **after M11 at the earliest**, since M13 (weighted graphs/shortest paths) is also arguably still "graphs." The README's own Pass 1c summary (line 31) preserves the original wording correctly. But `04-curriculum.md`'s handoff section (line 338, before this pass's fix) wrote: *"Per Pass 1c: Advent of Code... introduced only after M15... once arrays through NP-completeness are covered"* — both misattributing the "after M15" claim to Pass 1c (which said no such thing) and pushing the curriculum's only planned source of blind, untagged, interleaved practice out past M12–M15 (greedy, DP, NP-completeness) entirely. Whether the right target is M11 or M13, both are well before M15, so the drift finding holds either way. This matters concretely: it means the self-directed learner gets zero externally-authored, unauthored-by-them feedback until after the *entire* curriculum, instead of much earlier — which, given Finding 1 shows CP1/CP2/CP4(a) are compromised, would have been the first genuinely clean signal available. **Fixed directly in `04-curriculum.md`** (see below) — this is a documentation-accuracy correction of a verified drift, the same category of fix Pass 4 applied to Pass 3, not a structural redesign. `scripts/verify_sequencing.py` was re-run after this edit and after the Finding 2b edit above: still passes (both are prose changes outside the fields the script parses).

## The self-authorship problem, addressed directly (not just re-flagged)

Pass 4 asked Pass 5 to decide what to do about the M14 silent fork-callback and the checkpoints being contaminated for a self-directed learner who is also the author. Findings 1–3 show the contamination is worse and more specific than "the author knows the answers in general" — it's traceable to identifiable leaks (a README line, a reading-order choice, an assigned-reading overlap, six friction prompts, and a structural gap that let even the clean-looking ones be read out of order). That reframes the fix: some of it is repairable by editing the leaks, and what's left over needs a different self-check mechanism than "trust the checkpoint," not a better-worded checkpoint.

**Recommendation, in priority order (status: all four now acted on):**

1. **Demote CP1, CP2, and CP4(a) from stress tests to retrieval/formative checks.** They still have value as cumulative review (the paired review-checkpoint questions already do this job), but neither their prompts nor their answer keys should be read as evidence the curriculum caught a reasoning gap — for this learner, on this document, they can't. **Applied:** a designer note now says this explicitly at the top of `04-curriculum.md`'s Checkpoint answer keys section; CP4(a)'s learner-facing prompt is labeled a retrieval check directly (CP1 and CP2's prompts are left as-is — CP1 was never mislabeled as anything, and CP2's contamination is a document-leak problem fixed at its source, not a labeling problem, see Finding 1).
2. **Keep CP3 and CP4(b) as the real stress-test instruments among the four**, and add a discipline, not a rewrite: write down the full answer and reasoning *before* re-reading the relevant milestones or the answer key. This doesn't remove the fact the author designed the questions, but it does test whether the reasoning is retrievable and applicable under the encoding it was taught, which is closer to what these two checkpoints were meant to measure. **Applied:** added as a cross-cutting mechanisms bullet in `04-curriculum.md`, naming CP3 and CP4(b) specifically.
3. **Advent of Code (from M11 at the earliest, per the corrected sequencing above), used continuously through the rest of the curriculum rather than saved to the end, is the actual answer to the self-authorship problem** — it's the only material in this entire plan the author didn't write and doesn't already know the intended solution to. Kattis (untagged) follows once AoC feels easy, per Pass 1c, unchanged. **Applied** (see Corrections below).
4. **The M14 silent fork-callback stays exactly as Pass 4 left it** — a documented design decision for adapting this curriculum to a different learner, not a test this learner can take, per Pass 4's own honest caveat. Nothing in this pass's findings changes that conclusion; re-litigating it would be re-flagging, not deciding. **No action needed** — this recommendation was to leave it alone.

## Answer to Pass 4's question

Partial fail as originally audited, with a specific and repairable shape — and the repairs have now been made. The curriculum's *milestone-level* design overwhelmingly avoids the pattern-matching failure mode `00-philosophy.md` rejects — representation-first framing, cost-driven derivation, and invariant-based reasoning run through essentially all 16 milestones, and all 16 have a labeled consolidation step (Finding 0). But two separate layers meant to make struggle-before-instruction actually happen were both weaker than Pass 4's own text claimed: 6 of 16 milestones stated the remedy before or inside their friction prompt (Finding 2a — all 6 now rewritten to withhold it and consolidate it afterward instead), and none of them structurally protected even the other 10 from being read out of order (Finding 2b — fixed with an added rule). The layer Pass 4 proposed specifically to *verify* transfer happened at all — the four checkpoints — was compromised on 3 of its 4 instances by identifiable, fixable leaks (Finding 1): CP2's document-level contamination is now repaired at the source, CP4(a) is now honestly labeled as a retrieval check rather than presented as blind, and CP1 was never actually mislabeled (it's cumulative review by design, not a leak). Only CP3 and CP4(b) were ever real transfer instruments, and that hasn't changed — the fix was in what the document claims about the other two, not in making them into something they structurally can't be. The one instrument immune to the self-authorship problem entirely (Advent of Code) had drifted, undocumented, to the point in the curriculum where it could do the least good (Finding 3) — corrected to match Pass 1c's actual sequencing. None of this ever meant the curriculum failed at teaching first-principles reasoning — the friction/formalization *content* was real and mostly sound throughout. It meant the curriculum taught that reasoning better than it tested, or reliably delivered, whether it transferred — and Pass 4's own handoff had conflated those two claims by treating the checkpoints as an already-solved stress-test layer rather than something Pass 5 needed to check. With this pass's corrections applied, the two layers (friction-before-formalization, and checkpoint-as-honest-instrument) are now aligned with what Pass 4 originally claimed for them.
